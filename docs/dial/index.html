What am I looking at?

Grow makes a new cloud: starting from the ringed root, each generation adds independent Gaussian steps along a uniformly random tree (gold). Then the true tree fades, and the dial starts at the temperature that grew the cloud.

Draw two trees shows two independent exact samples from the law at the current temperature, over the faded edge probabilities: shared edges thick, the rest solid for one tree and dashed for the other. Their expected overlap is Σπ²/(n−1), and its standard deviation is at most 1/√(n−1) (Burton–Pemantle), so the fraction of shared edges is predictable even when the trees themselves are not.

A tree law on the points: a spanning tree T has probability proportional to exp(−λ Σ ℓ²) over its edges. It is the posterior over trees if the cloud was grown by Gaussian diffusion along an unknown tree, with step variance σ² = 1/(2λ). Hot: every tree is nearly equally likely. Cold: only the minimum spanning tree survives, and cutting its longest edges is single linkage.

Edge opacity is Pr(e ∈ T) = we·Reff(e) (Kirchhoff), computed exactly in your browser by a log-space Kron elimination. The dial is κ = λ × the mean squared MST edge. The Ultrametric view compares λ⁻¹ log Reff with the single-linkage ultrametric; their gap is at most 2 log n / λ for every pair.
